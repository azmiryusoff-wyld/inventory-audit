Help

The monthly routine, the words the app uses, and who does what.

The monthly routine

  1. Start the month on Month close (every outlet at once) or on Audits. Opening stock is copied from last month's closing count. In an outlet's first month in the app, upload last month's closing count from the old workbook as opening stock.
  2. Chef: upload the POS item sales report: the raw Dinlr Item Sales export works as it is (one location per file), as does the scripted sheet's summary. Link any POS names that aren't on the menu straight from the Data checks list. Upload this outlet's B2B sales invoices to sister brands (sets are turned into portions with the batch yield). Add wastage on Uploaded data.
  3. Procurement: upload the Xero supplier invoices (and inter-company invoices if any) and the closing stock count.
  4. Fix the data checks. Each says who fixes it and what it leaves out of the numbers. A single wrong line can be edited on Uploaded data.
  5. CEO: review and sign off. Signing off freezes the month; unlocking it lets it follow today's recipes and prices again.

Words used in the app

COGS
Cost of goods sold: what the ingredients and packaging used in the month cost.
Theoretical COGS
What it should have cost, from the recipes of everything sold, produced, transferred or wasted.
Actual COGS
What it did cost: opening stock + purchases − closing stock.
Variance
Actual minus theoretical. Positive means more was used than the recipes say (waste, over-portioning, theft, a missing count). Negative means less (often a missing purchase or a count error).
Tolerance
How big the F&B variance (actual − theoretical) may be, either way, as a share of sales, before the audit shows "Outside tolerance". Usually 2%: on RM 300,000 of sales, up to RM 6,000 either way.
Yield
The usable share of an item after trimming or cooking loss. 80% yield means 1 kg bought gives 800 g usable, so the recipe's net quantity is divided by 0.8.
Semi-finished item
Something made in-house from other items, such as a sauce, syrup or dough, with its own recipe. Every semi-finished item must have a recipe and a batch yield. It can be counted in stock by weight (Semi-finished Qty on the count sheet, in the unit its recipe makes); the app breaks the count down through its recipe into the supplier items it was made from, and adds them to those items' opening or closing stock. Count what is physically there, by weight, part-made batches included.
Substitution
A one-way swap of an item in every recipe, for example when a supplier's product changes.
Base unit
The unit recipes use for an item (g, ml, pcs). A pack holds a number of base units.
SST
Sales and service tax. Sales, the dashboard and menu engineering use POS prices with SST included; Menu costing takes SST out to compare cost with the net price.
Menu engineering classes
STAR popular and high margin · PLOWHORSE popular, low margin · PUZZLE high margin, not popular · DOG neither · NO RECIPE sold but has no recipe, so its cost is unknown.
Sign off
Freezes the month: its numbers stay as they were even if recipes or prices change later, and files can't be uploaded.

Who does what

  • Chef: menus, recipes, semi-finished items, substitutions, POS sales, linking POS names, wastage.
  • Procurement: purchases and stock counts. Can see menus and recipes but not change them.
  • CEO: everything, plus sign-off, brands, outlets, users and who sees which outlet (Admin).
  • Outlet manager: sees only the brands or outlets ticked for them.

Item prices, pack sizes and categories come from the central item master workbook, so change them there.
